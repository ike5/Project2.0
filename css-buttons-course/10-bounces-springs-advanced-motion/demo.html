Module 10 — Bounces, Springs & Advanced Motion

Overshoot + settle, everywhere. Hover and press the buttons; reload to replay the pop-in entrance. All effects degrade under prefers-reduced-motion.

1 · Bounce on hover — one ease-out-back transition

A single transition on cubic-bezier(0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1). The 1.56 shoots the value past the target, then it settles. Cheap, interruptible, physical.

2 · Settling bounce — decaying overshoots

Hover to drop it. Overshoots decay 45 → 18 → 7 → 2 %, so it reads as a ball losing energy and coming to rest — not a machine.

3 · Elastic pop-in entrance

Runs on load (reload to replay): scale 0 → 1.1 → 0.95 → 1 with a fade. Overshoot past 100%, dip under, settle. Staggered by 120 ms each.

4 · Springy press & release (:active)

Press: scales down fast (no bounce going in). Release: springs back out on the ease-out-back curve, popping past resting size. That contrast is what feels "clicky."

5 · "Juicy" tactile hover

Rises + grows + casts a bigger, softer shadow, all on one springy curve. The growing shadow is what reads as real elevation off the page.

6 · Jelly / wobble

Skew oscillation with opposing width/height scale — squash wide, stretch tall — preserving apparent volume. Playful and gummy. Kept under 0.6 s.

7 · Staggered icon + label reveal

Hover: the icon springs up, the label chases it 120 ms later. The button clips them (overflow: hidden) so they slide in from below. Stagger is the cheapest way to look expensive.

8 · Combined transforms — order matters

A "tada" pop stacks scale + rotate in one transform. Applied right-to-left in the previous function's space — reorder them and a spin becomes an orbit.
