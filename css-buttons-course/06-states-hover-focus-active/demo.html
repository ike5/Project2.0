Module 06 — States: Hover, Focus, Active & Disabled

Every interactive state, in the right source order. Tab with your keyboard to see :focus-visible; press and hold to feel :active.

1 · The full state matrix on one button

Hover it, press & hold it, tab to it, and compare with the disabled copy. One button, five states — resolved by source order: normal → :hover → :focus-visible → :active → :disabled.

2 · The touch problem: gate :hover

Left is gated with @media (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine) — on a mouse it hovers, on a phone it simply doesn't (so it never sticks after a tap). Right is ungated: on a touchscreen its pink hover will stick until you tap elsewhere. On a desktop they look the same — the difference shows on touch.

3 · :active — instant pressed feedback

Press and hold: the button nudges down 1px, scales to .98, and gets an inset shadow so it reads as pushed in. Small + fast = physical. This fires on touch too, which is why touch devices still feel responsive.

4 · :focus-visible ring (forced-colors safe)

Tab to these — the ring appears. Click them — it doesn't (that's :focus-visible). The right button uses a custom double ring built from box-shadow with a transparent outline fallback, so a real ring still shows in Windows High Contrast / forced-colors mode.

5 · disabled vs aria-disabled="true"

Try to tab to each. The real disabled button is skipped entirely (out of tab order, click blocked). The aria-disabled one still receives focus — so a keyboard user can reach it and read why — but note its click is not blocked by the browser (you'd block the action in code). Discoverability vs. hard-block: that's the tradeoff.

6 · Toggle button — [aria-pressed]

The aria-pressed attribute drives both the look and what a screen reader announces, so they can never disagree. Click to toggle on/off — on looks "sunken" and green. (One line of JS flips the value in production; the CSS keys entirely off the attribute.)

7 · Loading button — [aria-busy]

A CSS-only spinner (a rotating ring with one transparent edge), a dimmed label, cursor: progress, and pointer-events: none to stop double-submits. aria-busy="true" tells assistive tech the button is working. Reduced-motion slows the spin instead of freezing it.
