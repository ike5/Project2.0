Module 05 — Shadows, Depth & Neumorphism

Depth is light and shadow. Hover the lift cards, press the inset ones, and compare the two arrows to see why drop-shadow() exists. Tab through to keep the :focus-visible rings.

1 · box-shadow anatomy

One shadow, five values: offset-x offset-y blur spread color — here 0 6px 16px -4px rgba(0,0,0,.55). Positive offset-y drops it down (light from above); the -4px spread pulls it in so it only peeks out the bottom.

2 · Layer shadows for realistic softness

Left: a single shadow — flat and cheap. Right: a tight contact shadow plus a big diffuse one. Two layers is what real design systems ship.

3 · An elevation scale (levels 1–5)

Five reusable tokens (--e1…--e5). Offset, blur, and darkness all grow together — higher = floats farther and blocks more light. Apply depth by name, tune it in one place.

4 · Hover lift (translateY + bigger shadow)

Hover: the button rises (translateY(-4px)) and its shadow swaps to a higher elevation token. Motion + depth together read as "coming toward you."

5 · Pressed inset (sinks on :active)

Press and hold: the outer shadow disappears, an inset shadow carves a well from the top edge, and a 2px nudge down completes the sink.

6 · Glow — a colored shadow that matches the fill

The shadow color is the same custom property as the background, with a big blur and no offset so it radiates. Hover to intensify the halo. (Glow reads best on dark — which is exactly where we are.)

7 · Neumorphism (soft UI) — needs a matching background

This panel is #e0e5ec and so is every button on it — that shared color is mandatory. Shape comes only from a light shadow up-left and a dark one down-right. The middle button starts in its :active pressed state (both shadows moved inset); press the others to match it.

8 · drop-shadow() follows the shape; box-shadow doesn't

Both arrows are clipped with clip-path. Left uses box-shadow — it draws the shadow of the rectangle, leaking past the point. Right uses filter: drop-shadow() — it hugs the arrow's real silhouette.

9 · Cheap lift via a pseudo-element (perf tease → Module 14)

Same visual lift as card 4, but the big shadow lives on a ::after and we animate only its opacity — cheaper than repainting a box-shadow every frame. Why that matters comes in Module 14.
