Module 14 — Accessibility, Performance & Best Practices

Make expressive buttons correct. Toggle your OS "reduce motion" setting and reload. Tab through with the keyboard to see the :focus-visible ring. On Windows, try High Contrast Mode.

1 · Reduced motion — it calms under the OS setting

The button lifts on hover and the badge's dot pulses forever. Turn on Reduce motion (macOS: System Settings → Accessibility → Display; Windows: Settings → Accessibility → Visual effects → Animation) and reload — the lift is removed and the pulse stops. The color feedback stays.

2 · Cheap vs. expensive — what you animate matters

Both buttons grow ~20% on hover and look nearly identical. The left one uses transform: scale() — the GPU compositor handles it, no layout, no paint. The right one animates width/height, forcing the browser to re-run Layout (reflow) every frame. Open DevTools → Performance and record a hover on each: the right one shows purple "Layout" bars.

3 · Forced-colors safe (Windows High Contrast)

Under High Contrast Mode the browser discards custom backgrounds and shadows. A button whose shape was made of background alone can vanish. This one keeps a border and, in forced-colors mode, swaps to system colors (ButtonText, ButtonFace, Highlight) so it stays visible and its focus ring stays obvious.

4 · 44×44 hit targets & touch

The gear icon looks 30px, but the amber dashed box shows its real 44×44 tap area, projected with a pseudo-element — easy to hit with a thumb. The text button uses min-height/min-width: 44px. All carry touch-action: manipulation to kill the 300ms tap delay.
